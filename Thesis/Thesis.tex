	\section{Mission Profile} \label{se:mission_profile}
		% mission profile general
		A model of the mission profile was created to describe a potential mission with continuous position, velocity, and acceleration with respect to time. The mission profile is modeled as a series of mission profile segments. Constraints are put on the position, velocity, and acceleration at the nodes between mission profile segments. The remaining values at the nodes are calculated using basic kinematic equations. At values of time between mission segment nodes, the position, velocity, and acceleration are interpolated using cubic Hermite splines. [citation needed] Acceleration changes linearly with respect to time.

	\subsection{Thrust Calculation}\label{ss:thrust}
		
		A calculation of the thrust profile is required in order to preform the power and fatigue analysis. 
		
		The thrust equation over the duration of the mission profile is shown below. Both the acceleration and drag forces are considered.  		
		
		$$ F_\text{thrust}(t)= +-v(t)^2 * s_a * \rho * c_d + (a(t) + g) * m $$ \label{eq:thrust}
		
		The v(t) and a(t) represent the velocity and acceleration of the mission profile at given point in time. 
		
		$s_a$ is an estimate of the surface area of UAV. This is obtained by summing the projected surface of every beam and hub segment with the projected surface area of the two batteries. It is a function of thee design variables.
		
		$\rho = 1.2250\unit{\kilogram\per\meter\cubed}$ is the air density. $c_d = 0.256$ is the drag coefficient. [How to justify?] $g = 9.8066\unit{\meter\second\squared}$ was used for the gravity constant. 
		
		The mass is estimated as the sum of 45kg payload, the mass of the frame, and the mass of the two batteries.
		
		To simulate the pre-liftoff, and post-landing period, a rev up and rev down period were added to the thrust forcing function. This was modeled as a linear increasing force with respect to time up until the force required for take off for the rev up period. The rev down period was modeled as a linearly decreasing force until no force is applied. A duration of 5 seconds was selected for both periods.

	\section{Optimization} \label{se:optimization}
	
	
		The problem is formulated as follows:
		
		$$ \min \textnormal{mass}(d_a,t_a,d_s,t_s,r_s,t_f,t_w) $$
		
		subject to:
		
		$$g_j(x) \le 0; j = 0, 1, 2, ..., J$$
		$$x_i^L \le x_i \le x_i^U$$
		
		design variables: 
		
		\begin{itemize}
			\item $d_a$: arm diameter,
			\item $t_a$: arm thickness,
			\item $d_s$: strut diameter,
			\item $t_s$: strut thickness,
			\item $r_s$: strut distance from center,
			\item $t_f$: hub flange thickness,
			\item $t_w$: hub web thickness 
			\item $h_l$: hub length
		\end{itemize}
		
		derived variables:
		\begin{itemize}
			\item $d_{tip}$: Deflection at tip
			\item $\sigma_{max}$: Maximum stress from bending 
			\item $f_{amp}$: Amplitude of displacement at the tip of the uav arm
			\item $D$: Damage per mission
			\item $ E_{used}$: Energy used over th duration of the mission
			\item $ E_{total}$: Energy used over th duration of the mission
			
		\end{itemize}
		
		design parameters:
		\begin{itemize}
			\item $d_{allowable}$: Deflection allowable
			\item $\sigma_{allowable}$: Stress allowable from bending 
			\item $R_{allowable}$: Allowable amplitude displacement ratio
			\item $n_{missions} $: number of missions before damage
		\end{itemize}
		
		Constraints: \\
		\large
		
		Limit on deflection from bending by force required to lift the UAV. \\
		$$ g_1(d_a,t_a,d_s,t_s,r_s,t_f,t_w,h_l) = d_{tip} \le d_{allowable} $$
		
		Limit on stress from bending by force required to lift the UAV. \\
		$$ g_2(d_a,t_a,d_s,t_s,r_s,t_f,t_w,h_l) = \sigma_{max} \le \sigma_{allowable} $$
		
		
		Limit on amplitude of displacement given a forcing function based on the propeller speed of the UAV. \\
		$$ g_3(d_a,t_a,d_s,t_s,r_s,t_f,t_w,h_l) = \frac{f_{amp}}{d_{tip}} \le R_{allowable} $$
		
		
		Limit on the damage the UAV during a mission. \\
		$$ g_4(d_a,t_a,d_s,t_s,r_s,t_f,t_w,h_l) = D * n_{missions} \le 1 $$
		
		Energy
		$$ g_5(d_a,t_a,d_s,t_s,r_s,t_f,t_w,h_l) = E_{used} / E_{total} \le 0.8 $$

	\subsubsection{Displacement} \label{sss:displacement}
		
		The static FEA solver was used to calculate the maximum displacement magnitude. To simulate the loading at hover, the loading is located at the end of the UAV arm pointing upward while the center of the UAV is fixed in displacement and rotation. The load is a point load and is equivalent to the mass of the payload multiplied by gravity divided by the number of blades.  
		
		The formula for the constraint is shown in \ref{eq:opt_disp}. d_{max} is th maximum displacement of the UAV frame with the described loading. d_{allowable} is the maximum allowable displacement, 
	
		[show figure of loading?]
		
		$$ g_1(d_a,t_a,d_s,t_s,r_s,t_f,t_w) = d_{max} \le d_{allowable} $$ \label{eq:opt_disp}
		
		
	\subsubsection{Stress} \label{sss:stress}
		
		The loading described for the displacement constraint was also used for the stress constraint. The static FEA solver was used. The allowable stress is determined by the maximum stress of the material and a factor of safety.
		
		\sigma_{max} is the maximum nodal bending stress in the FEA beam system as calculated by the . \sigma_{yeild} is the tension yield strength of the material. \text{FOS} is the FOS associated with the stress constraint. 
		 
		$$ g_2(d_a,t_a,d_s,t_s,r_s,t_f,t_w) = \sigma_{max} \le \sigma_{yield} * \text{FOS} $$
		
	\subsubsection{Frequency} \label{sss:frquency}
	
		The objective of the frequency constraint is to ensure that the frequency experienced by the propeller is not near the natural frequency of the vehicle.
		
		Two analyses were done. The static analysis and the frequency analysis. The static analysis is equivalent to the on displacement constraint. A frequency analysis was done with a amplitude equivalent to the magnitude of the force in the static analysis. Formula \ref{eq:opt_freq} describes the ratio of the amplitude of displacement ($d_{amplitude}$) in the dynamic analysis to the resulting displacement of static analysis $(d_{static}$). The should be less than the allowable ratio ($R_{allowable}$). The magnitude of these force could be any number, the payload was chosen so the static analysis could be reused, saving computation time. [Justify??? Source??? ]
		
		$$ g_3(d_a,t_a,d_s,t_s,r_s,t_f,t_w) = \frac{d_{amplitude}}{d_{static}} \le R_{allowable} $$ \label{eq:opt_freq}
		
		
		The frequency of the oscillating loading function is described as two times the maximum rad/s of the propeller times the maximum percent throttle experienced during the mission profile. The forcing function is assumed to be twice propeller speed due to the propeller having two blades.
		
		This is used for the frequency of the oscillating force for the dynamic solver. The location of the force and boundary conditions are the same as the static analysis.
